Annual SFBAFCC Awards

Explore the annual selections of the SFBAFCC, highlighting the films that shaped each year's cinematic landscape.

  • 2025 — The Best Picture winner One Battle After Another stood out for its sweeping historical scope. Its ambitious narrative and emotional clarity made it a defining film of the year.
  • 2024 — Anora earned Best Picture with its bold, intimate portrait of desire and consequence. The film's fearless storytelling resonated strongly with the SFBAFCC.
  • 2023 — Christopher Nolan's Oppenheimer won Best Picture for its monumental historical scope. Its blend of intellectual rigor and emotional weight defined the year.
  • 2022 — The Banshees of Inisherin captivated critics with its darkly comic portrait of friendship and isolation. Its sharp writing and unforgettable performances made it a standout.
  • 2021 — The Power of the Dog won Best Picture for its stark psychological tension and masterful direction. The film's slow‑burn intensity left a lasting impression on the SFBAFCC.
  • 2020 — Nomadland resonated deeply with its poetic portrait of American itinerant life. Its quiet strength and humanistic focus defined the year's cinematic conversation.
  • 2019 — Quentin Tarantino's Once Upon a Time in Hollywood won Best Picture with its nostalgic, sun‑drenched vision of late‑60s Los Angeles. Its blend of revisionist history and character‑driven storytelling made it unforgettable.
  • 2018 — Alfonso Cuarón's Roma impressed with its visual elegance and emotional depth. The film's intimate storytelling and technical mastery defined the year.
  • 2017 — The Florida Project won Best Picture with its vibrant, empathetic look at childhood on society's margins. Its blend of realism and warmth made it a standout achievement.
  • 2016 — Barry Jenkins' Moonlight earned top honors for its groundbreaking triptych narrative. Its exploration of identity and vulnerability remains one of the decade's defining works.
  • 2015 — Spotlight was celebrated for its powerful depiction of investigative journalism. Its clarity, restraint, and moral urgency made it a critical favorite.
  • 2014 — Richard Linklater's Boyhood impressed with its unprecedented 12‑year production. The film's naturalistic storytelling offered a rare cinematic experience.
